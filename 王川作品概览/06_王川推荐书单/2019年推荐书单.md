# 2019年推荐书单

Date: January 6, 2020

Here is a list of interesting books I read in 2019. I choose them because they offered me a different perspective to look and interpret the world, and add new dimensions to my repertoires of mental models.

(1) The Starfish and the Spider: The Unstoppable Power of Leaderless Organizations By Ori Brafman, Rod A. Beckstrom , 2006

(2) The Ciano Diaries, 1939-1943 By Galeazzo Viano, 1943

(3) The Lords of Creation: The History of America's 1 Percent By Frederick Lewis Allen, 2014

(4) Nothing Like It In the World: The Men Who Built the Transcontinental Railroad 1863-1869, By Stephen E. Ambrose

(5) Genius in the Shadows: A Biography of Leo Szilard By William Lanouette, 2013

(6) The Power Paradox: How We Gain and Lose Influence By Dacher Keltner , 2016

(7) The Paradox of Choice: Why More Is Less By Barry Schwartz, 2009

(8) Wealth, War and Wisdom, By Barton Biggs, 2010

(9) The Case Against Reality: Why Evolution Hid the Truth from Our Eyes By Donald Hoffman, 2019

(10) Frames of Mind By Howard Gardner, 2011

(11) The laws of simplicity By John Maeda, 2006

(12) The War of the Soups and the Sparks: The Discovery of Neurotransmitters and the Dispute Over How Nerves Communicates By Elliot Valenstein, 2005

(13) The logic of political survival By Bruce Bueno De Mesquita, 2005

(14) Company of One: Why Staying Small Is the Next Big Thing for Business By Paul Jarvis, 2019

(15) Masters of the Word: How Media Shaped History from the Alphabet to the Internet By William Bernstein, 2013

(16) Medical Essays: "The young man knows the rules, but the old man knows the exceptions." By Oliver Wendell Holmes

(17) A Splendid Exchange: How Trade Shaped the World By William Bernstein, 2009

(18) Hoax: A History of Deception: 5,000 Years of Fakes, Forgeries, and Fallacies By Ian Tattersall , 2018

(19) The Half-Life of Facts: Why Everything We Know Has an Expiration Date By Samuel Arbesman, 2012

(20) Alchemy: The Dark Art and Curious Science of Creating Magic in Brands, Business, and Life By Rory Sutherland, 2019

(21) Range: Why Generalists Triumph in a Specialized World By David J. Epstein , 2019
